Local knowledge

Long Beach has its own permits, gas utility, and zoning map

Long Beach has its own municipal gas utility. Long Beach Utilities provides natural gas in Long Beach and Signal Hill and to parts of nearby communities, while Southern California Edison provides electricity. Gas and electric programs come from different places, so AIRPRO SOLUTIONS can help you sort out which ones apply before you choose equipment.

Permits run through the City itself. Long Beach issues building, electrical, plumbing, and mechanical permits through its Permit Center at 411 West Ocean Blvd., 3rd Floor, and permits and plans for mechanical construction on private property are issued and processed there, separate from LADBS in the City of Los Angeles. AIRPRO SOLUTIONS can walk you through what your project needs.

Two more local items are worth knowing. The City's Noise Control Program enforces Municipal Code Chapter 8.80 and names air conditioners among equipment that can exceed noise standards, so it helps to plan where new equipment will sit. The City's zoning and land-use map shows a property's zoning, historic-district status, coastal-zone status, and parking-impacted areas. Official links are below and in the section that follows.

Long Beach HVAC questions we hear most

Does AIRPRO SOLUTIONS service all of Long Beach? +

Yes. AIRPRO SOLUTIONS serves homes and businesses across Long Beach as part of our LA County coverage. Long Beach runs its own permit office and its own municipal gas utility, so we plan each visit around your specific address.

Where do I get an HVAC permit in Long Beach? +

Through the City of Long Beach Permit Center. The City issues and processes mechanical construction permits for private property at its Permit Center, 411 West Ocean Blvd., 3rd Floor, alongside building, electrical, and plumbing permits. Ask the Permit Center what your specific project requires. AIRPRO SOLUTIONS can walk you through the steps.

Does Long Beach regulate air conditioner noise? +

The City's Noise Control Program covers air conditioners. The program enforces Municipal Code Chapter 8.80 and names air conditioners and other equipment among sources that can exceed noise standards. Enforcement follows a written sworn complaint from a resident or property owner in the affected noise district. Check the current code text for exact limits before you decide where new equipment will go.

Who supplies gas and electricity in Long Beach? +

Natural gas comes from Long Beach Utilities, and electricity comes from Southern California Edison (SCE). Long Beach Utilities provides gas in Long Beach and Signal Hill and to parts of nearby communities, and operates more than 1,900 miles of gas pipeline. Because the two services come from different utilities, gas and electric programs are separate, so check each one before you buy.

Is there a rebate for AC or a smart thermostat in Long Beach? +

SCE lists Golden State Rebates instant coupons for air conditioners and smart thermostats at participating retailers. SCE also lists a $75 bill credit through eligible demand-response programs when it temporarily adjusts an eligible smart thermostat by up to four degrees, and free upgrades for eligible households through its Energy Savings Assistance Program. Long Beach Utilities lists natural-gas efficiency programs as well. Terms and eligibility can change, so confirm current details with SCE and Long Beach Utilities before you buy.

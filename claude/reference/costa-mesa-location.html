Local knowledge

Costa Mesa's single-family Insta-Permit, multifamily housing, and noise limits

Costa Mesa has a Residential HVAC Insta-Permit through its online TESSA portal, but the City limits it to a single-family dwelling on a single parcel. Its scope can include A/C condensers, forced air units, furnaces, heat pumps, mini splits, and new ductwork, and the City says exterior equipment must be installed at ground level on the side or rear of the home, where it is not visible to the public. Work beyond that scope goes to a Mechanical - Residential permit application, so AIRPRO SOLUTIONS confirms which permit path applies with the City before work starts, including for condos, townhomes, and apartments.

The City's About page lists ZIP codes 92626, 92627, and 92628 and describes Costa Mesa as 16 square miles, just one mile from the Pacific Coast. SCAG's local profile, using California Department of Finance data for 2018, counts 42,836 housing units: 39.6% single-family detached, 10.2% attached, 13.3% in buildings of 2 to 4 units, 34.7% in buildings of 5 or more, and 2.2% mobile homes, which makes about 48% of housing multifamily. The same profile, citing the 2017 American Community Survey, reports that 49.6% of housing was built before 1970. These figures are dated, so we confirm the equipment, access, and ductwork at the property, and in a multi-unit building we confirm equipment access and placement.

Municipal Code Sec. 13-280 sets an exterior noise standard for all residential property of 55 dB(A) from 7 a.m. to 11 p.m. and 50 dB(A) from 11 p.m. to 7 a.m., reduced by 5 dB(A) for impact, simple tone, speech, or music sounds, with additional duration tiers. Sec. 13-281 sets interior limits of 55 dB(A) by day and 45 dB(A) at night. The code has no section written for HVAC equipment, and the text we read comes from a third-party code host that may not reflect the most current legislation, so we confirm how the limits apply to your address before choosing a condenser location. The City's notice says it adopted the 2025 California Building Standards Code with local amendments, effective January 1, 2026.

For commercial work, the City's General Plan Land Use Element describes South Coast Plaza, along the Bristol Street corridor, as the largest single concentration of retail uses in the city, a 92-acre East 17th Street commercial corridor, and a Westside with an intertwined mix of industrial, commercial, and residential uses. The City lists a separate Mechanical - Commercial permit application. Utility and state incentive programs change, so we link the SCE, SoCalGas, TECH Clean California, and The Switch Is On pages rather than quote amounts. Confirm which utility serves your address and check current eligibility before you buy. Official links are in the card beside this text.

Does AIRPRO SOLUTIONS service all of Costa Mesa? +

Yes. AIRPRO SOLUTIONS serves homes and businesses across Costa Mesa as part of our Orange County coverage. Costa Mesa takes permit applications through its online TESSA portal, so we plan each visit around your specific address.

Do I need a permit to replace an AC or furnace in Costa Mesa? +

Check with the City before work starts. Costa Mesa offers a Residential HVAC Insta-Permit for a single-family dwelling on a single parcel, issued by email within minutes of payment, and a Mechanical - Residential permit application for work beyond that scope. Applications are entered directly in TESSA, and the City says paper forms are ignored. We do not assume a replacement is exempt, so confirm your project's permit path with Building Safety at 714-754-5273. AIRPRO SOLUTIONS can walk you through the steps.

Can a condo, townhome, or apartment use the Insta-Permit? +

The Insta-Permit is limited to single-family homes. The City describes it as a limited permit for a single-family dwelling on a single parcel, and says work beyond that scope goes to a Mechanical - Residential permit application. For a condo, townhome, or apartment, we confirm the permit path with Building Safety before work starts.

Where can the outdoor unit go under the Insta-Permit? +

Outdoor equipment goes on the ground at the side or rear of the home. The City's Insta-Permit page says exterior equipment must be installed at ground level, with no roof-top equipment, on the side or rear of the building, where it is not visible to the public. All components must be in a privately owned location, and the City's standard plans for HVAC must be used.

What does the City require of a contractor who pulls the permit? +

The City says contractors need an active State contractor's license and a Costa Mesa business license. Owner-builders use a separate Owner-Builder application. Insta-Permit requests are submitted through TESSA.

What does the City ask to see with an HVAC replacement permit? +

The City's handout for replacing mechanical, electrical, and plumbing fixtures lists Title 24 energy forms and a 24 by 36 inch site plan showing the equipment location and property line. Requirements vary by project, so confirm yours with Building Safety at 714-754-5273 or buildinginfo@costamesaca.gov.

Which building codes apply to HVAC work in Costa Mesa? +

The City's notice says Costa Mesa adopted the 2025 California Building Standards Code with local amendments, effective January 1, 2026. The notice describes Ordinance 2025-08 as adopting the code by reference. We confirm current requirements with the City for your project.

Does Costa Mesa have a noise rule that affects AC equipment? +

Costa Mesa has general residential noise limits. Municipal Code Sec. 13-280 sets an exterior standard of 55 dB(A) from 7 a.m. to 11 p.m. and 50 dB(A) from 11 p.m. to 7 a.m. on residential property, reduced by 5 dB(A) for impact, simple tone, speech, or music sounds. Sec. 13-281 sets interior limits of 55 dB(A) by day and 45 dB(A) at night. The code has no section written for HVAC equipment, and the text we read is from a third-party code host that may not reflect the most current legislation, so we confirm how the limits apply to your address before we choose a condenser location.
